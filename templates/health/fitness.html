What this is, and is not

Where the figure comes from

{{ assessment.vo2max }} ml/kg/min, recorded {{ reading.date }}, source {{ assessment.source }}. Google derives a demographic estimate from resting heart rate, age, sex and weight. No run, no treadmill and no gas analysis went into it.

What the percentile means

Of people of your age and sex in the reference population, about {% if assessment.position == "inside" %}{{ assessment.percentile|floatformat:0 }}%{% else %}this many{% endif %} have a lower VO2 max. The reference cohort was measured on a treadmill; your figure is modelled, so read the placement as approximate.

How far the source is checked

The table is the Cooper Institute Aerobics Center Longitudinal Study as published in ACSM's Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription, 11th ed., Wolters Kluwer, 2021, Table 4.7. The values here were transcribed from a secondary rendering of it, and two of the 84 entries were spot-checked against a second rendering. Nobody has compared them against the printed edition, so treat this as an unverified transcription rather than a checked citation.

Both numbers, one cohort

The percentile and the fitness age are read off the same table. An earlier version of this page took the percentile from Cooper and the fitness age from a Norwegian cohort whose means run about six ml/kg/min higher in the same age band, which let it call one reading above average for its age and older than its age at the same time.

It is not a health check

Cardiorespiratory fitness is one measure among many, and a percentile against a reference cohort is not a diagnosis, a risk score or a medical opinion. Nothing here should be used to decide anything clinical.

The norms this was read against

VO2 max in ml/kg/min for {{ assessment.sex }}s. Your bracket is marked. The fitness age above is the 50th percentile column of this table, anchored at the midpoint of each band and interpolated straight between those anchors; only the anchors are published figures.
